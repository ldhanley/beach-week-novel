Chapter 1

END OF SCHOOL

“Learning is not child's play; we cannot learn without pain.” - Aristotle

Some kids love school; I was not one of them. But there was a time at the very end that stands out. It was a time of intense joy but also confusion, a time of realization that an end was near but also a beginning, and most importantly it was a time for all the hot girls in the school to dawn bikinis and head to the beach. School was over, and the last few moments of our innocent youth were to be spent forming indelible memories, memories of Beach Week.

More than two decades have passed since my high school days, and most of my memories of high school have faded. But I remember every moment of Beach Week spent playing on the beach with friends from school. I also remember the people that weren’t able to make it. I was almost one of those people, but things fell into place for me in a most unexpected way. My name is Dave and this is my story about my first few steps into adulthood and a glimpse behind the curtain for those who were unable to go to beach week.

The week before graduation, Brandy’s mom finishes a phone call with her friend. She calls for Brandy, “Brandy, come in here.”

“What is it, Mom? I will be right in.”

“Some of the parents said that Tina’s mom is renting a beach house for next week.”

Brandy pauses for a second and says, “Yeah, and she invited all the girls from class, all the girls that do well in school to stay there.”

“Brandy, are you planning to stay there?”

“Yes, but I will make sure that everybody is doing the right thing.”

Brandy’s mom smiles and turns her head, “Honey, we’ve taught you everything we know about leading a good life. One of the hardest things in life is learning how to deal with temptation, especially when you are young and there is peer pressure involved. Just make sure that whatever decisions you make, you have a clear head, and that you make them with your future in mind.”

Brandy feels a knot in her stomach. She knows her mom is talking about beach week, but her thoughts are elsewhere. She begins to smile thinking about sunning at the beach with Tina.

“Now, Brandy, I trust that you will do the right thing, but I am not sure about all of your classmates. I just want you to know that I will call the other parents to make sure that everything is okay.”

Brandy nods her head and says, “Mom, I love you. I’ve been very lucky to have such great parents. Not only will I try to do the right thing, but I will also try to be an example for my classmates.”

The doorbell rings, “Mom, Justin is here. I’ve got to go.” Brandy turns and heads out the front door.

Brandy’s mom closes her eyes and takes in a deep breath. Eighteen years and nine months ago, she had her own beach week. She then dials the phone to talk with Tina’s Mom but Tina answers. “Hey Tina, this is Mrs. Myers. May I please speak with your mother?”

Tina yells, “Mom, Brandy’s mom is on the phone.”

It’s the standard hypnotism act. I expect he’ll make them act like chickens, but Virgil has something else in mind.

“Six? Now, count the fingers on your left hand starting with six.”

“Six, seven, eight, nine, ten.” Mike again looks puzzled.

“Ok, now count your fingers on both hands.”

“One, two, three, four, six, seven, eight, nine, ten… eleven?” He says this without any hesitation between four and six.

“Eleven? Now Mike, have you always had eleven fingers?”

He shakes his head and says, “No.” The audience laughs and Mike looks around confused which causes everyone to laugh again.

Maybe this hypnotism stuff works. If he can get someone to forget the number six, maybe he can get me to forget about my freshman year. If that works, then my sophomore and junior years could come next.

“Ok, Mike, the number ‘five’ now exists. Give Mike a hand.”

Virgil dismisses the two other students and then looks at Amy and asks her, Mike, and Jake a couple of questions. Amy responds but what they are saying is not audible. Mike and Jake get up and bring their chairs over to an open area and place the chairs facing each other. Virgil then positions the chairs to have about a three foot separation. My stomach starts to tighten.

“For our final demonstration, we are going to explore the mind’s ability to control the body. Amy, lie down on these chairs.” Amy walks over and lies down with her shoulders on one chair and her ankles on the other. Virgil then takes his shoes off. I look over at Amy’s Mom. She is near panic. Amy’s Dad places his hand on her arm.

“Your stomach muscles are getting tighter, tighter, they are becoming like steel. Your muscles are so tight that they are steel.”

Someone behind me says, “What’s he doing? Is he going to stand on her?”

Stand on her? Of course he’s not going…

Virgil the Great then proceeds to step onto Amy with both feet and stands straight up on her unsupported stomach. Amy’s mother shouts “Get off my baby!” Unfazed, Virgil continues standing on Amy and then after a few moments steps off. The audience applauds but it is subdued. This has to be the most amazing trick I’ve ever seen.

We split up into three man teams and play a half court game. Some of the guys seem surprised that I can play. Disengaged is too weak a word to explain my high school experience, massively depressed is closer to the mark.

We finish up the game and stroll back to main building to dance with some of the girls who are dancing to music. The senior prom was just a few weeks earlier, and it feels like the energy from the prom is back. Amy even ends up dancing with me as the last song plays, and somehow I end up outside talking with her.

Then more classmates come out and small groups form with Amy disappearing into one of the groups. Did she just invite me to stay with her and a bunch of girls for a week in a beach house? My friend Aaron comes out of the building looking for me. We’ve only become friends in the last year or so. Like Mike, we never had a class together until this year.

On the drive back to Aaron’s place, I realize that this is the last time I will see most of my high school friends. Those first awkward moments of freshman year seem like only yesterday. Every friend before was lost. The lesson learned from my childhood was to only form weak bonds. As soon as someone was my friend, they were gone forever. Maybe my relationship with Aaron will be different.

After a few minutes into the drive, Aaron says, “Come crash at my place tonight. Steve is coming over. We can stay up tonight and watch the sun rise over the beach.” Aaron’s parents are renting a beach condo outside of town. It is down the beach from some resort hotels loaded with young women looking to have a good time.

“Awesome. Maybe we can stop off and get some subs.”

“No need, we have some homemade pizzas in the fridge.”

Pink Floyd plays on the radio as we drive on the empty dark streets. It’s a drive that I have made many times before, but this night it is different. We enter a long wooded stretch of road.

“Dave, you like Amy. Don’t you?”

“Yeah, I do.” I answer not thinking. He could have asked about any other girl in our school, and I would have answered the same. The question asked is not the one answered. I speak the same words as everyone else in my school, but not the same language. I like all the girls in my school but know that any close relationship would end with me being crushed.

We arrive at Aaron’s place well past midnight and Steve pulls in behind us. Aaron’s parents let him stay in the downstairs part of the condo. It has a bunk bed, but we don’t plan on sleeping that night. We head out the back onto the beach.

Steve asks “Are the twins still here?”

“No, they went back to Iowa.” Steve shakes his head at the lost opportunity.

We sit down on the beach facing towards the water. The moonlight dances on the water to the soft rhythm of waves. Staring out into the darkness the gentle breeze pulls me into another state. We talk for hours, but as soon as something is said, it is gone. This moment exists. I feel nothing but peace. It is never ending. A lifetime could be lived within it, but then it’s gone in an instant.

The sun rises and I become aware. The only things of importance in this life are unchanging. They are unknowable. They cannot be taught. They exist now and have always existed. They bind us to those who’ve come before and connect us to those yet to come. As our footsteps in the sand are washed away, others take its place until the water is no more. To deny this is to fight the waves, a struggle to preserve a single breath which only stops the next.

Steve breaks my trance, “Let’s get some breakfast.”

I’m so relaxed but at the same time am exhausted. “You guys go, I’ll see you at Alexander’s tonight. I need some sleep.”

Aaron gets into Steve’s car. I see them drive off, but I don’t move. There’s something stopping me, something I must know. A conflict exists. I’m old enough and reasonable enough to realize the conflict but am incapable of understanding its inescapability and inevitability. Aaron’s my friend, Steve and Aaron are friends, but Steve and I have no real relationship. It’s not that I dislike Steve, he’s an interesting guy. He’s open and willing to discuss anything. He might even feel the same about me. We’re just not friends.

I’m not sure if there is something wrong with me, or if I am just different. Most people seeing their best friend going off with another friend might be jealous or angry. If I feel anything, it is closer to peace of mind. Of course, I could be lying to myself. Maybe I am jealous, which yields the conflict. But, I don’t think that’s it.

Friendship is a fleeting gift. A friend helps you become a better person. A friend will help you to become a better friend to other people. If someone truly is a good friend, then your goal can only be to help them. I guess that’s it. I don’t fear the loss of Aaron as a friend, I’m happy that he and Steve are close. Having had many best friends in my life, I am glad that Aaron is now one of them, even if this week is our last.

THE LOFT

“A true friend is one soul in two bodies.” - Aristotle

“Dave, the phone is for you.” My sister yells from downstairs.

“Ok, I’ll be right down.” Still lying in bed, I am not sure I can get up. I’ve been so high on life the last few weeks of school that I’ll probably have a hangover soon, but not this week.

“Shower? You’re going to the beach. The salt water will take care of you. Just get out here.”

“Ok, I’m coming…”

I get my clothes together and walk by the hallway mirror. I look like total crap. It’s at least a thirty minute drive to get Aaron. What’s the difference if I take a shower? I need to take a shower. We’re not going to get any leeway hanging with Alexander’s crew. This brings up a point of confusion to ponder in the shower.

I’m not sure why Amy invited me to stay with the girls, but it is at least understandable. I’m nonthreatening and can sometimes be funny, but how did Aaron get an invite from Alexander? Alexander has never liked me, but this is due to my laughing at his idle threats. I’ve never sought social standing, so he has no power over me. But if there is one person he dislikes more than me, it is Aaron. So I’m not sure what to expect from this weekend, but it doesn’t matter as Monday is coming soon.

I pick up my clothes for the weekend and nod goodbye to my sister. Only moments away from my house, the automatic pilot part of me takes over the driving freeing me to focus on more important things such as my plans for the upcoming week. A conversation begins in my head.

“Dave, why are you so excited?”

“I don’t know; I just am.”

“It’s only a week at the beach.”

“I know, but that’s not it.”

“What is it, Dave?”

“I’m not sure; this is new to me. I’ve never been this excited by a social event before.”

“What do you mean, Dave?”

“I’ve gone to parties in high school before, but no matter how much fun, they always sucked, as if people were just faking that they were having fun.”

“I’m not sure I understand.”

“What’s to understand? It was like being a prisoner with a life sentence and enjoying a single moment of freedom, only to be returned to the cell with no chance of escape the next morning.”

“I’m still not sure I understand. What’s different this time?”

“What’s not different? School’s over; I’m no longer forced to do things that I don’t want to do?”

“Are you sure that is it, Dave?”

“No, but it has to be part of it.”

“What else is it, Dave?”

“Well, there is something else. Amy invited me to stay at the girl’s beach house next week.”

“That’s interesting. Is it Amy’s beach house?”

“No, but…”

“Did she invite you to stay with her in the beach house?”

“Well, not exactly. She invited me to stay at the beach house. I’m pretty sure her boyfriend will be staying with her.”

“I see.”

“What? What do you see?”

“I understand.”

“You do? Good, now tell me.”

“You’re confused.”

“Brilliant. Thanks.”

“You’re not sure if your invite to stay at the beach house is legit.”

“No, Amy invited me. But I don’t know if the other girls know. But it’s not like Alexander’s.”

“Alexander’s?”

“Yeah, we’re crashing at his place tonight, and I know it’s not legit. Besides, we’re at Aaron’s house so you can go away.”

Aaron comes out of his house loaded with stuff. I pop the trunk, and we manage to jam everything in somehow. Aaron gives me a big smile and we slap hands. He says, “Let’s do this!”

Chapter 9

THURSDAY MORNING - THE AFTERMATH

Morning after the keg party disaster. I wake up on Mike's couch with a crick in my neck and the taste of regret.

Bright sunlight through the windows feels like an accusation. Highlighting every mistake from last night.

Richey's crashed on the floor beside me. Still fully dressed. Snoring softly. Few other refugees scattered around. Aaron on another couch. Steve curled up in a chair too small for him.

Last night comes back in waves. The fight. The sirens. Our panicked escape. Brandy breaking down on the sidewalk. Our perfect week imploding.

"Upstairs trying to clean up. But Dave, we have a bigger problem." Her voice drops to a whisper. "The owner showed up an hour ago. She's furious. My mom told her it would just be family, not teenagers having parties."

My stomach sinks. "What did she say?"

"She wants everyone out by noon. And she's talking damages. Possibly lawyers." Tina's eyes fill with tears again. "I don't know what to tell my mom. This is going to ruin everything."

I look around at the destruction. Try to think of something helpful. But the truth is, there might not be a way to fix this. Some mistakes are too big. Too public. Too permanent.

"Let's focus on what we can control," I say finally. "We can clean up. Get everyone out safely. Figure out the rest later."

Chapter 10
